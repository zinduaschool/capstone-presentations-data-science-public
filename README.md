# Scoring Rubric

## Assessment Overview

The Presentation Rubric assesses how well you communicate, explain, and defend your project to an audience. It applies to:

- **End of Module presentations**
- **Capstone presentations**

This rubric is used alongside the technical rubric for your course. The technical rubric scores the work you submit on the LMS; this rubric scores the presentation of that work. The two scores are recorded separately.

The assessment covers:

- Problem and context
- Story structure and flow
- Visual communication
- Demonstration of the work
- Technical understanding and explanation
- Insights, impact, and reflection
- Questions and answers
- Delivery

**Total: 100 Points**

---

# 1. Problem & Context - 15 Points

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Problem or research question clearly stated | 3 |
| Why the problem matters, grounded in a real business, social, or personal context | 3 |
| Audience, stakeholders, or users identified | 2 |
| Data narrative given: where the data came from, the population it covers, and its context | 5 |
| Scope and goals of the project made clear | 2 |
| **Subtotal** | **15** |

Always start with the data narrative. For software and engineering projects, describe the users, inputs, and systems involved instead.

---

# 2. Story Structure & Flow - 15 Points

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Clear beginning (context), middle (work and findings), and end (conclusion or call to action) | 4 |
| Logical flow from one point to the next | 4 |
| Presentation is concise; report-level detail is summarized for the audience | 4 |
| A summary walks the audience through the main points | 3 |
| **Subtotal** | **15** |

A presentation is not the report read aloud. Keep the detail in the submission and bring the audience the story.

---

# 3. Visual Communication - 15 Points

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Slides are clean, readable, and uncluttered | 3 |
| Charts use data labels, annotations, and captions that state the takeaway | 4 |
| Pre-attentive attributes (colour, size, position) direct attention to what matters | 3 |
| Illustrations used to explain key concepts, model workings, or system architecture | 3 |
| Results displayed visually rather than only in tables | 2 |
| **Subtotal** | **15** |

Every key concept in the project should be explained with an illustration. Place explanations where the eye naturally lands, such as a caption directly below the chart.

---

# 4. Demonstration of the Work - 10 Points

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Live demo, or clear evidence of the working solution | 5 |
| Demo focuses on the most important functionality or findings | 3 |
| Demo runs smoothly, with a fallback if something fails | 2 |
| **Subtotal** | **10** |

Examples of evidence include a deployed app, a published dashboard, successful pipeline runs, or model predictions on new data.

---

# 5. Technical Understanding & Explanation - 20 Points

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Learner explains every step, method, and decision in their own words | 8 |
| Key concepts used in the project are explained correctly | 4 |
| Choice of tools, techniques, or models justified against alternatives | 4 |
| Results and metrics explained, including what they mean for the audience | 4 |
| **Subtotal** | **20** |

Understanding is weighted above polish. Work that the learner cannot explain, including work produced with AI tools, will not earn full points.

---

# 6. Insights, Impact & Reflection - 10 Points

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Key findings or outcomes clearly communicated | 3 |
| Recommendations or next steps for the audience | 2 |
| Limitations and potential pitfalls acknowledged | 2 |
| Challenges faced and lessons learned shared | 3 |
| **Subtotal** | **10** |

---

# 7. Questions & Answers - 10 Points

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Questions answered accurately | 4 |
| Questions answered confidently and directly | 3 |
| Learner acknowledges what they do not know, and how they would find out | 3 |
| **Subtotal** | **10** |

---

# 8. Delivery - 5 Points

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Confident, clear delivery that holds the audience | 3 |
| Presentation delivered within the allotted time | 2 |
| **Subtotal** | **5** |

---

| Score | Performance Level |

## Applying the Rubric

### End of Module Presentations

- Present the module project, focusing on the concepts covered in the module
- A demo may be a walkthrough of the notebook, dashboard, or application
- Reflection should cover what was learned in the module and what the learner would do differently

### Capstone Presentations

- Present the full capstone project end to end
- A live demo or evidence of the deployed solution is expected
- Follow any time limit set in the course's capstone brief (e.g. 10 minutes for Big Data Engineering)
